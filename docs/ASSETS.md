# Asset provenance

The integrated game includes all 74 playable card rows from the archived roster and uses simulation rules version 41 for new battles. The checked-in generated manifests are the authority for the current asset inventory; incremental counts in the feature history below describe earlier imports. Current outputs include 74 native card portraits, 49 animation families, 208 effect exports, 78 combat-audio profiles and 250 combat sound groups. Source metadata, source configuration, inferred scheduling and browser balance choices remain distinct; none establishes current live-game balance or exact original runtime parity.

`src/game/archived-card-stats.json`, `archived-card-vitals.json`, `archived-card-splash.json` and `archived-collision-radii.json` hold the source-derived projections used by the current rules. Their importers retain the pinned CSV bindings; the engine selects older values for recorded rules versions. Card detail panels read the current effective arena stats, including when a historical deck is inspected.

## Barbarian Hut

`npm run assets:barbarian-hut` pins the archived `BarbarianHut` Rare card and building rows: seven elixir, 1,100 source hitpoints, one-second deployment, 60-second lifetime, and two Barbarians per wave, released 500 ms apart with a 13,500-ms spawn pause. Each Barbarian retains its source 1,000-ms deployment and 400-ms delay. The browser scales the Hut to the roster's level-11 Rare health curve (2,816 HP) and uses its existing level-11 Barbarian stats. `SpawnCharacterLevelIndex=2` is retained but does not separately override the normalized unit level.

The browser starts the first wave after the spawn pause, releases the second Barbarian 0.5 seconds later, and waits 13.5 seconds after that second unit for the next wave. Source rows give the pause and interval but do not specify first-wave initialization or exact left/right release offsets; those remain reconstruction choices. Destroying the Hut cancels an unfinished pair. Both one-frame native team exports, the `barbarian_hut` portrait, `goblin_hut_deploy` and `goblin_hut_die` effects, and their original sound groups come from the pinned archive. `reference/client/barbarian-hut-manifest.json` hashes the source and generated rules.

## Tombstone

## Freeze card

`npm run assets:freeze` pins the `Freeze` spell and area-effect rows from the retained 1.8.0 logic tables. They specify four elixir, Epic rarity, a 3,000-unit radius, a 4,000-ms area lifetime and buff duration, enemy-only targeting, and hits on ground, air, buildings and hidden targets. The browser uses these as a three-tile, four-second persistent area at its normalized card level. Archived per-level duration increments are not modeled. `reference/client/freeze-manifest.json` records source and output hashes.

The native `freeze` portrait comes from `ui_spells`; the cast cue resolves to `freeze_04.ogg`. `scripts/import-destruction.mjs` retains the `Spell_freeze_ground` and `Spell_freeze_loop` particle compositions, and `scripts/import-effects.mjs` retains both teams' 390-frame, 30-FPS ground and radius exports. Each cast produces the 40-particle ground burst and 70-particle snow composition while the authored ground/radius artwork advances with the area. Particle placement, overlap and the browser's normalized four-second lifetime are reconstruction choices; retained client tables do not prove the original effect scheduling.

The pinned `character_buffs.decoded.csv` maps both `Freeze` and `ZapFreeze` to `sc/effects.sc` export `filter_cold`. `npm run assets:cold-filter` validates those bindings and retains the complete 29-frame, 60-FPS additive blue color sequence in `src/render/cold-filter-data.json`. Source and output hashes are recorded in `reference/client/cold-filter-manifest.json`. The browser loops the short clip while a troop is stunned. Clip repetition and composition order with deployment/charge filters are presentation choices; the archive does not establish their scheduling during simultaneous buffs.

## Poison card

`npm run assets:poison` pins the archived Epic spell's four-elixir cost, 3,500-unit radius, eight-second area, 250-ms area hit interval, one-second lingering buff, 57 source damage per second and 40% Crown Tower damage. The area targets enemies on ground and in air, including exposed buildings but excluding hidden Teslas. The browser's level-11 roster uses 121 damage per second, scaled approximately by its 689-to-325 Fireball damage ratio; this is a balance normalization, not a sourced Poison level. Overlapping clouds refresh one buff per entity without stacking. `reference/client/poison-manifest.json` hashes the source rows and generated rules.

The native portrait and small card icon are pinned to the same asset revisions as the existing roster. `Spell_poison_effect` supplies the one-shot skull emitter and `sound_poison_spell` impact cue; `Spell_poison_loop` supplies the 15-skull and nine-large-skull emitters. Multipart native buildings and towers apply the same color timeline to every layer; only troops apply the source squash matrix.

These are archived Clash Royale 1.8.1 assets; the Wizard and Goblin Stadium scenery use 1.8.0. They are historical assets, not the current game client. `scripts/import-sprites.mjs` uses the shared bounded SC1 reader in `scripts/lib/sc.mjs`. It preserves full walk, idle, attack and Prince charge sequences at their native 24–30 fps, nine source directions with left-facing mirroring, nested display-list transforms, source UV polygons and color transforms. Balloon uses its full multipart sequences with a single facing. Skeleton art is shared between teams; other families retain distinct team exports. Baby Dragon exports are explicitly selected because its source file also contains Inferno Dragon art. Native source textures are bundled as lossless WebP. Geometry is reconstructed and cached per unique frame in the browser; no four-frame sampling or fixed 128-pixel cells remain. The native SC color transform layout is documented in [SupercellFlash](https://github.com/sc-workshop/SupercellFlash/blob/main/supercell-flash/source/flash/transform/ColorTransform.cpp).

`scripts/compose-scene.mjs` reconstructs first-frame display lists, resolves child clips and transform matrices, triangulates source UV polygons and produces tower and scenery textures. The format reference is [Galaxy1036/sc_decode](https://github.com/Galaxy1036/sc_decode/blob/master/sc_decode.py). Scaling is 1/1024 for matrix coefficients and 1/20 for vertex/translation coordinates. The retained per-export JSON files record bounds, origins and triangle counts. This is static asset reconstruction; no original game executable was run, and it does not establish original runtime behavior.

`reference/goblin-arena.csv` retains the historical 1.8.0 `assets/locations/goblin_arena.csv` placement table from the same archive commit. It was decoded as LZMA with the archived four-byte length field expanded to the standard eight-byte field. Stadium decorations use those coordinates at 1,000 coordinate units per tile. The renderer uses a 30-pixel art scale per tile; this display scale is a reconstruction choice.

The surrounding 3D geometry, fallback troop models, particle renderer, interface implementation and simulation code are created for this project. Original projectile/electrical/impact exports are retained from the same 1.8.0 archive's `assets/sc/effects` and `effects_tex.png`. `reference/sc/effects.bin` stores the source metadata; `scripts/import-effects.mjs` reconstructs 60 selected exports from that sheet plus three from `building_mega_bomb`, preserving their source parts, UV polygons and color transforms. `public/assets/effects/manifest.json` records source, metadata, output and texture hashes. This game implements a classic eight-card format with normalized, curated card stats; it does not claim live balance parity.

## Native screen-shake investigation

`reference/client/screen-shake-native-probe.json` retains nine Hopper evidence records from the pinned 1.8.0 x86 `libg.so` (SHA-256 `a7eb74c5b72770e39b8dc2c55e452fc11434f3df3efc46fd0ea13d28c1da9309`). `node scripts/probe-screen-shake.mjs /absolute/path/to/libg.so` verifies that hash and regenerates `screen-shake-layout.json` with ELF32 load mappings, the position-independent string reference, float constants, scale pointer and trigonometric import names. This was read-only static analysis; the original executable was not run.

The `ShakeScreen` string at `0x615a48` is referenced through a GOT-relative instruction at `0x1eb3e2`, explaining why the previous direct string-reference probe missed it. The column loader stores its index at GOT+`0x1940`; the effect loader reads the integer into object offset `0x60`, and getter `0x1ebdb4` returns it (evidence IDs `ev_d06c1bf83cfe4971193ed4b9c8514a27c29bcd692d0a911c117132616c9b9be5`, `ev_396d82db9eb2c7895030a94256a49c65b9bb49d6cf0d792e7a3f80927d6b0e93`, `ev_1112650942af51d0adddfa76e283add335c737f9b0b15dacf919a51675637c29`).

The effect constructor at `0x77b7e`, gated by its final flag, multiplies this value by the float 0.001 and calls `0x8bc36`. That function replaces the existing scene timer only if the new value is greater. The update function at `0x8bdb0` clears its two offsets, computes a displacement when both frame delta and timer are positive, then subtracts the frame delta and clamps the timer to zero. This supports a millisecond duration with maximum overlap, rather than additive impulses (evidence IDs `ev_f9d863f9f3044839171e35acdca6370918fc2460c3ef634b2a919d42e345d155`, `ev_965453e326aa3ea4436c5720cc0749587f576d8d4a0f93eab836eb2bfc215134`, `ev_b5caa0c65930fb5c79014e3133d84ddecfda05398948e8d13e0a134aa913f3e3`).

The observed displacement radius is remaining time × 0.5 × a referenced scene factor, whose file-backed value is 12. An integer returned by `0x7c716(10000)` is multiplied by approximately 0.0006283, then passed to the imported `cosf` and `sinf`; the resulting offsets are passed with each child to `0x314fa0`, which stores them in child offsets `0x1c` and `0x20` through `0x314ef0` / `0x314f70`. The three additional records are retained in `screen-shake-offset-probe.json`; downstream transform consumption is not traced. The wrapper calls `0x35c670`, whose complete random-distribution semantics were not examined. No shake duration is added from a row's `Time` in the observed constructor. Runtime writers of the scene factor, all constructor flag callers, native frame cadence and the browser display-unit conversion remain unverified. The browser adaptation is described below.


`screen-shake-scalar-probe.json` retains six additional records tracing the effect scalar read through `0x1e49ea`, `0x351560`, `0x352230`, `0x34e9e0` and the integer-vector accessor. A missing-cell sentinel becomes zero. The original CSV loading/blank normalization was not fully traced. Both King effects place their sole authored `ShakeScreen=2000` on continuation row 1 while row 0 is blank. The browser deliberately retains that unique group value; `screen-shake-manifest.json` records both continuation-row interpretations. This is a documented reconstruction choice, not a claim that the unexecuted client resolves that blank identically. Multiple different values in one group fail import instead of being guessed.

`npm run assets:screen-shake` resolves camera effects from the existing source effect roots, including enemy substitutions, and hashes eight inputs plus its generated data. Fireball uses 0.8 seconds, Zap 0.25, Balloon contact 0.35, Balloon bomb and Princess Tower destruction 1, and King destruction 2. A group's shake starts at effect creation; its particle/sound row `Time` does not delay the group timer. The maximum overlapping remaining duration wins. Radius is remaining time × 6 native points, with the retained angular factor and sine/cosine displacement. A separate seeded cosmetic generator replaces the original untraced RNG; battle RNG and simulation IDs are untouched.

The browser converts native points through the renderer's established 30-points-per-tile art scale and applies a screen-plane orthographic projection offset.
