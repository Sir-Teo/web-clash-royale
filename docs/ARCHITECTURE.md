# Architecture

The game is a static Vite app. No server, official API, game account or secret is used.

## Battle simulation

`src/game/engine.ts` advances at a fixed 30 Hz. World coordinates span 18 × 32 tiles, with the blue player at the bottom. The river divides the battlefield at y=16. Ground units route through x=4 or x=14; flying units and the Hog Rider can cross directly.

Each player has an eight-card deck, a four-card hand, a four-card queue, capped elixir and crowns. Commands validate elixir and placement before mutating the hand or creating entities. Units acquire legal targets, move, attack and create projectiles. Spell and projectile damage resolve on impact. Buildings decay; the Witch summons skeletons; Prince charges; Balloon has death damage. The King Tower activates when damaged or after losing a Princess Tower.

AI uses the same command and resource path as the player. It scores affordable cards against visible threats, defends air with appropriate cards, and starts pushes when it has enough elixir. Training delays decisions. It does not receive extra elixir or deploy into illegal territory.

An initial seeded shuffle plus timestamped commands makes battles replayable. Replays disable AI decisions and consume the recorded commands. Particle randomness stays outside the simulation. Unit tests compare full entity, player and projectile state between a live run and its replay.

New battles use rules version 5, extending version-2 combat with Tesla concealment, delayed King activation and tower outcomes resolved at the end of a simulation step. Attack animations begin a pending windup; melee damage or projectile launch occurs when it completes. Target death, leaving attack range, stun and movable-unit knockback cancel that pending hit. Troops reconsider a distant tower destination when a defender or building enters sight, while retaining an engaged target. Projectile speed and homing are per-unit; Bomber throws retain their original landing position. Fireball travel time depends on distance and its radial knockback respects heavy-unit immunity. Arrows deliver three equal waves 0.2 seconds apart. Zap interrupts windups and Prince charge.

The original combat loop is frozen in `updateLegacyCombat()` for version-1 replay compatibility. A captured v1 match is compared against its original complete saved state. A captured version-2 match also retains its original complete state, including always-exposed Teslas. A captured version-3 match preserves immediate King activation. The captured version-4 match preserves delayed activation and the original immediate finish order. Current replay tests reproduce entire version-5 matches, pending attacks, projectile flight, Tesla exposure and King wake state. Profile history accepts all five versions and rejects unknown future versions.

Version 5 defers tower victory until every melee release and projectile impact in the 1/30-second simulation step has resolved. Both Kings falling in that step produces a draw; one fallen King takes priority over Princess Tower losses. Each defeated side's remaining Crown Towers receive death events without awarding extra crowns. Princess rewards are capped at three, and zero-value reward events are omitted. Equal tower trades at the regulation boundary or during overtime keep the battle running. Once a result is set, later steps and commands remain frozen. This simultaneous-impact window is this reconstruction's deterministic rule, not a measurement of Supercell's server timing. Earlier replay versions preserve their original scoring and finish order, including the historical same-step four-crown bug.

`scripts/import-combat.mjs` derives the compact `src/game/combat-data.json` table from pinned historical CSVs. Sight ranges, homing flags and knockback immunity are direct source fields. First-hit timing (`HitSpeed - LoadTime`) and the speed-to-tile conversion are reconstruction interpretations; damage, health and hit intervals remain this project's curated level-11 values. The implementation is deterministic, but is not a claim of exact original runtime behavior or current balance parity.

Tesla becomes untargetable and immune to damage while fully underground; lifetime decay continues. Ground units can pass over the closed hatch. Enemies within attack range trigger emergence, and departing enemies trigger an idle grace period followed by retraction. An exposed or partially exposed Tesla can take damage; stun freezes its transition. The 0.8-second rise, grace and retract intervals interpret the archived `UpTimeMs`/`HideTimeMs` values and native animation lengths. They are reconstruction choices, not measured original runtime behavior.

A King activates when it takes damage or loses a Princess Tower. Version-4 and later battles use the archived `KING_ACTIVATE_TIME_MS` value of 3,300 ms before starting its normal attack windup. Subsequent damage does not restart the wake sequence; stun overlaps the wake timer and still blocks attacks when present. Earlier replay versions keep immediate activation. `scripts/import-combat.mjs` derives this setting from the retained `globals.csv` alongside the combat profiles.

## Rendering

`src/render/arena.ts` projects the board with an orthographic Three.js camera. Static opaque geometry is batched by material. Animated source sprites share native textures, materials and lazily cached frame geometry. The importer preserves complete directional clips, child transforms and per-part color transforms. Sprites follow movement direction on bridges; attacks use full source sequences; Prince uses its separate charge animation and Balloon retains its multipart motion. Each Princess Tower uses its dedicated native Tower Princess character with both team colors and nine attack directions. It shares the animation library without adding a playable card to the roster; an Archer remains the asset-loading fallback. Camera-facing tower and scenery artwork keeps the original perspective. Locally constructed models remain available as asset-loading fallbacks.

Rendering interpolates positions independently of simulation steps. Health labels use the camera projection. Pointer coordinates are raycast onto the arena floor and passed to the same placement validator as AI commands. Preview ghosts use that validator, too.

Standing tower/building artwork is composited above opaque terrain without depth testing against the arena floor. Its authored pivot lies inside the image, so a normal billboard would otherwise lose its door, ramp or base below the floor. Transparent sprite sorting still uses the original arena position. Native buildings and static artwork fallbacks use the same rule, including placement previews.

Princess Towers reuse the King asset's native sheet and materials, with a separate geometry cache at their own world scale. Their base, attached character and foreground battlements share one billboard plane. Small depth offsets preserve the source layer order without drawing the foreground above unrelated arena objects. The dedicated Princess retains the original zero origin; its vertical placement uses `AttachedCharacterHeight` interpreted at 1,000 units per tile and projected through the arena camera. Static artwork remains the loading fallback. The additional geometry cache is disposed separately from shared textures/materials.

Deployment previews use the same `deploymentPositions()` formation as troop creation, including the complete 15-unit Skeleton Army and board-edge clamping. They share native animation geometry but own their translucent materials, keeping the actual troops opaque. Buildings use the loaded source artwork. Each lane's prohibited overlay follows its surviving Princess Tower, and the river stays blocked. Inline placement feedback combines territory and elixir checks, updates as elixir regenerates, and is announced as a polite status message. Dragged card portraits hide over the board so they do not cover the native formation. Pointer ownership prevents a second touch from changing an in-progress drag.

`src/render/effects.ts` reuses the native frame geometry and SC color-transform shader for 45 original effect exports. `src/render/projectiles.ts` draws source arrow, spear, cannonball, bomb and flame art along simulation flight positions, with trajectory-dependent billboard rotation and per-shot heights. Arrow waves are separate visible volleys. Tesla hit events retain the firing position so their authored beam joins the correct endpoints; Zap uses the original bolt and ground glow. Transient native effects follow a render effect clock that respects replay speed and pause and keeps advancing after victory so the final collapse settles. They share cached geometry/materials. Events retain their simulation time so effects start at the correct age when multiple steps precede a render; expired impacts are skipped. This rendering metadata does not change replay rules.

`src/render/buildings.ts` shares the native frame geometry and shader with troop sprites. Cannon chooses among 19 source aim angles per team and mirrors leftward shots. Tesla maps simulation exposure to the 39-frame source timeline using its authored deploy, idle, appear and hide labels. Its health bar follows the raised tower. The King uses its 98-frame activation timeline, separate 86/99-frame blue/red idle loops, and 36 half-turn cannon directions with leftward mirroring. Separate base, cannon and foreground meshes preserve source occlusion while allowing independent aiming. Wake frames follow the simulation countdown; replay seeking reconstructs the matching pose. Building previews share these assets; source textures and all frames are available offline.

Tower destruction is separate from combat state. `src/game/arena-layout.ts` supplies the same ordered six tower sites to the engine and renderer, preserving legacy IDs. Missing/dead towers produce the appropriate native rubble; rebuilding a replay renderer reconstructs ruins from current entities. Rubble is projected onto the arena floor with its authored screen proportions, avoiding billboard clipping below the floor.

`src/render/crowns.ts` uses the native 16-frame crown rotation for screen-space reward flights. Crown events include the newly earned count, so a direct King win produces three while a King destroyed after one Princess Tower produces two. This is presentation metadata and does not alter replay rules. Flights start from the projected tower position and end at the current DOM score counter. Their clock follows the other effects, including pause, replay speed and post-victory time. Positions and art bounds are recalculated on resize; expired effects and replay resets release their owned materials while preserving shared native geometry. The result panel waits 1.4 seconds of rendered effect time for the last crown. Its pending presentation is bound to the completed battle and is consumed by the frame loop, so backgrounding freezes the entire ending sequence and changing battles cancels the old presentation.

`src/render/destruction.ts` uses 23 retained death compositions and 21 emitters. Tower collapses contain 78 particles; troop compositions preserve distinct elixir bursts, dust, clouds, ground marks and the Knight's lingering sword. Recursive source effects retain delays, team substitutions, component scale and renderable scale. A separate seeded cosmetic RNG preserves variety without changing the simulation.

Particles are grouped by native frame and render layer into instanced meshes. Each instance retains its own transform and fade opacity through an extension of the SC shader; native frame geometry and textures remain shared and intact. Instances sort back-to-front within a batch. Ground marks project onto the arena floor while other particles face the camera. The 1,024-particle cap bounds simultaneous effects. The frame-geometry pool evicts unused batches above 96 entries and releases all batches at rest; clear/reset also disposes the owned materials. A 60-Skeleton inspection scene retains all 960 particles in 11 native particle draw calls at 0.3 seconds, down from 960 separate draws. This measures draw calls rather than physical phone frame rate.

Paused updates reuse existing instance buffers when no particles were added and the camera orientation is unchanged. New bursts and camera rotation invalidate that cache even with zero elapsed time. Empty scenes still advance the effect clock without rebuilding particle groups.

Analytic trajectories make motion independent of render frame rate; exponential drag, ground settling, air height and world scaling are reconstruction choices. Transparency sorting across different frame batches is approximate. Reset/replay seeking clears transient particles. Finished matches stop producing projectile trails and allow the last impacts/debris to expire.

## Interface and persistence

`src/main.ts` owns lobby, deck editing, battle controls, results and replay views. During battles, a viewport-height flex layout gives the arena all remaining space after the header and hand; short landscape screens use a two-column arena/hand layout. Safe-area insets, dynamic viewport height and touch targets support phone use without scrolling the match. Card hand elements update only after the hand changes. Health, timer and elixir update at about 10 Hz. AI battles and replays automatically pause when the page is hidden or enters page-cache suspension. Friendly battles continue on their server. The animation-frame loop is cancelled while hidden and restarted with a fresh timing baseline on return; the match remains paused until the player resumes. Lobby, paused and finished views render at 30 FPS, while active matches follow the display refresh rate. Troop animations and scenery use the match clock during battles/replays, so pause and replay speed affect them consistently. The FPS diagnostic reports frames actually rendered over a sampling interval. Modal dialogs pause a live AI match and resume when dismissed; friendly battle menus leave the server running.

`src/game/profile.ts` validates versioned localStorage data and recovers from corrupt or unavailable storage. It retains three decks, results for ten matches, trophy/gold counters, audio preferences and four chest slots. Card levels are normalized to level 11; progression does not alter battle stats.

`src/ui/announcements.ts` selects native announcement frames directly from simulation time. A cached signature avoids DOM changes while paused; seeking resets the previous clock and restores the correct frame without replaying skipped cues. SVG text uses the archived Supercell Magic font and retained source transforms/colors; the elixir icon uses the cropped native texture. Responsive bounds keep the overlay inside the arena in portrait and landscape. A regulation winner at exactly 180 seconds leaves the clock at zero and suppresses overtime; tied battles enter the two-minute overtime sequence. Existing replay rules remain unchanged.

Countdown voices are emitted once when a displayed second crosses 10 through 1. The overtime jingle follows the 180-second boundary; discontinuities larger than half a second are silent. The simulation already caps each rendered update at 15 fixed steps. `GameAudio` tracks active sound instances, removes ended/rejected playback, updates playback rates with replay speed, and stops effects on pause, mute, backgrounding or navigation. Match start/end horns use source sound mappings. Result music begins with the result panel after the final crown animation.

`src/ui/intro.ts` owns a separate presentation clock for the 134-frame, 30-FPS native battle opening. Frame 103 is the retained `actionFrame` label: before it, the clock consumes elapsed presentation time and hands zero time to the simulation. When an update crosses that boundary, only the remainder advances combat. The first 3.433 seconds therefore consume no match time, elixir regeneration or AI decisions. The last 31 frames can overlap active play. Pausing/backgrounding freezes this clock; replay speed scales it. Seeking clears the intro, and replay restart creates a new opening without changing replay data or rules versions. Near-boundary floating-point values snap to the exact authored times so fixed-step updates release combat on frame 103.

`src/ui/native.ts` shares SVG text, polygon, texture and color-transform rendering between announcements and introductions. Each multipart ribbon retains its own polygon layers; constant-UV polygons sample their source color instead of attempting a singular affine transform. The intro keeps all native frames and shapes, substitutes player/arena labels, leaves unused visitor text empty, and hides the league placeholder for unranked Arena 1. Long text is fitted to its original text-field width after loading the native font; it remains escaped literal text. The intro overlay clips its offscreen entrances/exits inside the arena and does not intercept pointer controls. Hand buttons and deployment paths remain disabled until the action frame. The obsolete generic FIGHT/REPLAY message renderer was removed.

`src/ui/elixir.ts` connects eight retained HUD clips to the blue player's actual resources and deployment events. The native bar is rendered once with named SVG groups; subsequent updates stretch its center strip, move the curved cap and dividers, and update the integer display. Selected cards use the source eleven-frame cost outline. Regeneration pulses, four bounded overlapping spend floats, full-bar flow/warnings and x2/x3 badges use simulation seconds. The regeneration badge sits beside the battle clock, leaving all hand-card labels clear. Paused input produces no HUD DOM mutations. Seek resets transient feedback instead of replaying historical spending. The meter exposes its value, cap, regeneration multiplier and selected cost through ARIA.

The native display-list parser preserves each child's instance index as well as its asset ID. This distinguishes the nine dividers, which all reference the same shape. Runtime HUD layout supplies the dynamic dimensions absent from the archive. Tracks use 400 source units normally and 200 below a 280-pixel container width; only the track and cost geometry shrink, preserving the drop and numeral size on short landscape phones. A ResizeObserver records the new width, and the next presentation update applies it without changing game state or resizing inside the observer callback. Shared SVG rendering supports named binding groups and an optional outer geometry transform.


`src/ui/cards.ts` renders source card layers through the shared SVG display-list renderer. Per-card views substitute the unframed portrait at a reserved shape binding; each mounted artwork host has unique SVG clip/filter IDs and a cached render signature. Hand/deck/detail/next/drag presentations share the same assets while retaining their existing buttons, pointer ownership, ARIA labels and hit areas. Drag art is built only when its card changes, so pointer movement updates position without rebuilding the SVG.

A four-slot presentation clock tracks newly drawn cards independently of hand DOM replacement. Only changed slots play the 60-FPS source reveal; unchanged cards remain still. Animation uses simulation time, pauses with the battle, respects reduced motion and resets on replay seeking/restarting. Loading all native images and the local font precedes the switch from fallback art. Failed loading leaves the previous playable UI intact. `loadedCards` exposes the number of ready portraits through read-only diagnostics.

## Native health displays

`src/ui/health-data.ts` separates health fill selection from animation time. Its flattening helper substitutes the chosen 100-state bar frame into the source root while composing its matrices and colors. `healthSelection` derives team, source bar size, visibility and the King’s opening frame from entity state. Paused rendering and replay seeking therefore reconstruct the current display without replaying historical transitions.

`src/render/health.ts` preloads the shared atlas, tower crops and native font. Troop health uses cached GPU geometry with one shared texture/material; only the two team-specific Level 11 glyphs are rasterized into the reserved atlas band. Billboard meshes retain screen-readable sizing as the orthographic camera fits a phone viewport. Death and scene rebuilds remove their meshes; library disposal releases shared GPU resources. Missing assets retain the previous health bars.

Six tower HUD hosts use the shared native SVG renderer and accessible meter values. Cached artwork signatures and position transforms avoid DOM writes while paused, while a resize recalculates placement. The renderer initializes newly created visuals at each entity’s actual position before interpolation, fixing older troops appearing at the origin when a replay or paused scene is rebuilt. Read-only `loadedHealth` and `health` diagnostics report readiness and visible troop HUD states.

## Native timer and crown counters

`src/ui/hud-data.ts` derives the timer's outgoing/incoming frame from regulation/overtime time and composes each crown's independent frame into the original portrait or landscape display list. `CrownFeedback` tracks awards on the renderer's presentation clock, with explicit reset on match/replay start and seeking. Using one clock throughout the battle preserves the winning award when simulation time stops but post-match effects continue. The timer itself remains tied to simulation time.

`src/ui/hud.ts` preloads the native font and all cropped artwork before replacing the fallback HUD. Cached signatures avoid rebuilding unchanged SVGs. Timer semantics and crown totals remain accessible; native timer artwork hides the duplicate fallback text from assistive technology. The existing elixir badge remains a separate sibling. Media-query changes select the source landscape layout, and reduced motion uses stopped timer/crown frames. Crown flights target the rendered native icon bounds, falling back to the old score element if assets are unavailable. Read-only diagnostics expose `loadedHud` and the current crown/layout state.

## Native match ending

`src/ui/ending-data.ts` composes both final-score panels with independent empty, old and newly earned crown states. Only crown events from the finishing simulation tick trigger the new-crown animation. `EndClock` plays the complete 66-frame match-over clip followed by the complete 102-frame score panel at 30 FPS. Its visible-frame clock freezes when the animation loop stops in a background tab and continues after simulation has ended. Reduced motion uses a still score for 1.4 seconds; seeking to a completed replay holds that still score until another seek or reset.

`src/ui/ending.ts` preloads five native clips, their lossless crops and the local font. It caches unchanged frames, fits player names within source text bounds and preserves accessible final totals. The main view binds each ending to its completed Battle instance so repeated snapshots cannot restart it. Results wait for both the ending and the 1.4-second crown-flight deadline. Continue becomes available after that flight deadline. Replays never open the reward modal; restarting, rewinding or leaving clears the presentation. Missing native assets retain the existing result flow. Read-only diagnostics expose `loadedEndings` and the current ending state.

## Current boundaries

- AI and private two-player rooms are implemented. Account synchronization, public matchmaking and cross-server room discovery are not implemented.
- 24 curated cards; evolutions, champions, equipment and the full live roster are not implemented.
- Historical art and curated normalized stats; no claim of current balance parity.
- Local browser progress only; clearing browser storage removes it.
- The surrounding diorama, some placement/pathing details and effects are approximations.

## Authoritative friendly rooms

`server/rooms.ts` attaches the `/battle` WebSocket endpoint to either Vite's development server or the production HTTP server. Each room owns a 30-Hz `Battle` with AI disabled. Both ready checks start the same native intro countdown. Room membership selects a player's team; submitted cards are resolved against the server hand and normal deployment rules. The client cannot submit a clock, damage or elixir value. Snapshots are sent at 15 Hz and after accepted moves, with opponent hand/queue/elixir masked until the final replay. Input sequences prevent duplicate spends.

`src/network/client.ts` exposes the room API and persists reconnect credentials in session storage. `src/network/transport-worker.ts` owns the same-origin WebSocket, native ping/pong handling, application heartbeats and bounded retries through `client-core.ts`, with timers separated from graphics work on the UI thread. WebKit can still route worker socket callbacks through that thread. A three-second UI pulse identifies a blocked delivery path: application ping/check callbacks wait until the UI resumes, then allow one delivery turn for queued messages. This avoids false quiet-connection closes and queued ping bursts; responsive clients retain the 15-second quiet deadline, and server native heartbeats and reconnect grace are unchanged. Each connection generation rejects stale worker messages. State delivery permits one in-flight snapshot and retains only the latest pending snapshot until the UI acknowledges receipt; room, welcome, error and reaction messages remain ordered and are not coalesced. Worker creation/load failures fall back to the direct client, reusing the reserved seat from memory even when storage is unavailable. That fallback does not provide protection from a blocked UI thread. Reloading the same tab automatically resumes its saved room unless the URL explicitly invites the player to a different room. `src/network/perspective.ts` rotates red's coordinates and swaps presentation colors without altering canonical server commands. This includes projectile destinations/origins, troop positions, event positions and tower lanes. Replay metadata records the viewing team; a red replay runs the original canonical engine and projects its state into the rendered battle. No combat rules version change is needed.

`src/bootstrap.ts` and `src/network/session.ts` restore a saved connection before importing the graphics-heavy main module. Startup waits for welcome/error or an eight-second connection fallback, buffering only the latest room, snapshot and error. The main view then takes over the same client and consumes those messages; its initial lobby reset preserves the reserved seat. Fatal errors discard buffered state so an expired session cannot initialize a stale battle. Failed graphics downloads offer a reload action, and service-worker registration handles a main module that finishes after the document load event.

Client heartbeats distinguish a quiet server from a delayed browser event loop. If the heartbeat callback itself was delayed, queued socket messages get one interval to run before liveness is judged again. A normally running but silent connection still closes after the original message deadline; a stalled, still-silent connection closes at the next ordinary heartbeat. Server reconnect grace and authoritative match timing are unchanged.

Vite's JavaScript module preloads are disabled so a failed graphics download can recover on reload in affected WebKit builds ([WebKit 270357](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=270357)). The tiny entry imports the session client normally, and then dynamically imports the main view. Vite still loads the view's CSS before running it. The browser regression exercises the built server so this bundling behavior is covered.

Online menus do not pause the simulation, and the server continues while a browser is backgrounded. Lost connections receive 30 seconds to resume; explicit surrender/leave and timeout resolve on the server. Friendly records keep the opponent name and room identity, suppress ranked rewards and deduplicate reconnects to a finished match. Room state is ephemeral and single-process. Deployment, lifecycle and validation details are in `MULTIPLAYER.md`.

## Native King emotes

`src/ui/emotes.ts` owns the touch selector, mute preference, two independent presentation clocks and optional voice cleanup callbacks. `emote-data.ts` composes source bubble/cell placeholders with the selected native expression/icon. A bubble expires after its full 145-frame timeline while its nested expression loops independently. Paused solo battles hold their poses; hiding the document clears transient reactions and voices instead of replaying them on return. Lobby, replay, seek and match-ending transitions reset both bubbles. Reduced motion uses a stationary bubble and expression for the same display duration. Asset failures retain usable text reactions.

`src/game/emotes.ts` supplies the four expression kinds, six fixed quick-chat phrases and shared cooldown. Text reactions use the two native text-bubble timelines and the archived SC CCBackBeat font without adding a King voice. The room server accepts a kind only from a room member during countdown or play, derives the sender team from that membership, and enforces the 2.5-second cooldown independently for each player. The additive `quick-chat` message carries a validated phrase key; it uses the same player cooldown timestamp as `emote`, so alternating message types cannot bypass rate limiting. An omitted kind retains the original protocol's laugh behavior; malformed kinds are ignored without consuming the cooldown. Clients translate the canonical sender team into their own presentation perspective. No command or replay format changes are required.
